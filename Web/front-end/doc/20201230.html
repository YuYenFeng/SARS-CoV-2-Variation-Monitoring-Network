The UK Variant of SARS-CoV-2 (Official Name: VOC 202012/01): Q&A

Updated: 2020-12-30

SARS-CoV-2, the virus that causes COVID-19, has profoundly changed societies around the world. Using data from the Global Initiative on Sharing All Influenza Data (GISAID), Academia Sinica analyzed more than 200,000 viral genome sequences and found that the virus can be classified into six major types. Type VI began rising rapidly in many countries in February and March 2020, became dominant by April, and now accounts for 99% of circulating viruses.

However, the virus is still mutating. What is the current status of the UK variant that has recently drawn so much attention, and how might it develop? Here we try to answer these questions, and we will continue to follow the evolution of SARS-CoV-2 through this monitoring network.

Q1. Where did the UK variant come from?
Where was it first detected?

Q1. Where did the UK variant come from? Where was it first detected?

According to a report by Public Health England, the UK variant VOC 202012/01 carries 23 mutations in its genome. It was first detected in Kent, in southeast England, and its origin is still unknown. It has now been found in London and in southern and southeastern England.

Because VOC 202012/01 involves many mutation sites, institutions have used different criteria to decide whether a strain belongs to VOC 202012/01 (Note 1). Using the representative VOC 202012/01 strain EPI_ISL_601443 as the reference, and based on 265,138 viral genome sequences in GISAID, we found 1,354 VOC 202012/01 strains from the UK and 3 from Denmark. The first strains in these two countries were collected on September 20 and November 9, respectively. Its incidence in the UK began rising at the end of October 2020.

According to the Public Health England report, the new UK variant VOC 202012/01 carries 23 mutations, comprising the 17 mutations listed in Table 1 of the reference and the 6 mutations described in the text above Table 1.

The 17 mutations in Table 1 are as follows (nucleotide changes are given first, with the corresponding amino-acid changes in parentheses):

  1. 3 single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) in ORF1ab: C3267T (T1001I), C5388A (A1708D), T6954C (I2230T).
  2. 1 deletion in ORF1ab: nucleotides 11288–11296 deletion (SGF 3675–3677 deletion).
  3. 2 deletions in the S protein: nucleotides 21765–21770 deletion (HV 69–70 deletion) and nucleotides 21991–21993 deletion (Y144 deletion).
  4. 6 SNVs in the S protein: A23063T (N501Y), C23271A (A570D), C23604A (P681H), C23709T (T716I), T24506G (S982A), G24914C (D1118H).
  5. 3 SNVs in ORF8: C27972T (Q27stop), G28048T (R52I), A28111G (Y73C).
  6. 2 nucleotide variants in the N protein: 28280 GAT->CTA (D3L), C28977T (S235F).

In addition, the 6 synonymous SNVs described in the text above Table 1 are:

  1. 5 SNVs in ORF1ab: C913T, C5986T, C14676T, C15279T, C16176T.
  2. 1 SNV in the M gene: T26801C.

Because VOC 202012/01 involves many mutation sites, institutions may not use the same criteria to decide whether a strain is VOC 202012/01. For example, GISAID's description focuses on the 8 mutations in the spike protein. As another example, VOC 202012/01 belongs to lineage B.1.1.7 in GISAID, whose representative strain is EPI_ISL_601443 (September 20), so some definitions use the 17 mutations listed in Table 1 of the report.

We compared these criteria against 265,138 viral genome sequences in GISAID. Using only the 6 SNVs in the S protein, we found 1,460 VOC 202012/01 strains from the UK, 3 from Denmark and 1 from Australia, with the first strains in these three countries collected on September 20, November 9 and November 30, respectively. Using only the 2 deletions in the S protein, we found 1,463 strains from the UK, 13 from Denmark and 1 from Australia, first collected on September 20, October 12 and November 30, respectively; there was also one strain from Slovenia collected on March 7 (a data problem in GISAID cannot be ruled out, but if the data are correct, this mutation occurred very early). Using the 2 deletions plus the 6 SNVs in the S protein, we found 1,445 strains from the UK, 3 from Denmark and 1 from Australia, first collected on September 20, November 9 and November 30, respectively. Including all 17 mutations in Table 1, we found 1,354 strains from the UK and 3 from Denmark, first collected on September 20 and November 9, respectively. Finally, when all 23 mutations were included, the closest match was EPI_ISL_601443 (September 20), which carries all 17 mutations in Table 1 but only 5 of the 6 synonymous SNVs: at nucleotide 16176 it carries C rather than the variant T. A possible explanation for why no strain carries all 23 mutations is that VOC 202012/01 belongs to GISAID lineage B.1.1.7, with EPI_ISL_601443 (September 20) as its representative strain, and the missing mutation is found in other strains of the same B.1.1.7 lineage.

Q2. Will the mutations affect vaccine effectiveness?

VOC 202012/01 is not a new virus. It is still SARS-CoV-2, only with changes in its genome sequence. It has been found mainly in the UK and later in other countries such as Denmark and Australia. Recent news reports describe detections in Germany, Singapore, Hong Kong and elsewhere, but these have not yet appeared in the GISAID database. This is because not every virus sample is sequenced, sequenced data are not always uploaded to GISAID, and the criteria and definitions used to identify the new UK variant may differ (Note 1).

Q3. How should we monitor viral mutations?

Q3. How should we monitor viral mutations?

Errors made when a virus replicates are called mutations. If a mutation has no effect on the virus, it simply drifts within the viral population and has little impact. If it harms the virus, it is eliminated from the population. Conversely, if a mutation helps the virus adapt to its environment or to conditions in the human body, the variant will multiply and spread more easily among people. Most mutations fall into the first two categories (no effect or a negative effect). This variant appears to spread more easily, but the reason is unknown, and positive selection by the environment or within the human body cannot be ruled out.

The virus enters cells mainly through its spike protein, the key it uses to unlock and invade human cells. The receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the spike protein is the first part of the virus to contact the surface of human cells. In theory, changes in this region could slightly increase the virus's chance of invading cells or of evading vaccines, but this still needs to be verified.

The new UK variant has 23 mutations in its genome, 8 of which (2 deletions and 6 single-nucleotide variants, SNVs) are in the spike protein. One of these 6 SNVs lies in the critical RBD, the region where SARS-CoV-2 binds to human cells and which is key to the virus entering host cells. It requires continued monitoring.

Q4. How does it differ from previous variants?

Q4. How does it differ from previous variants?

Viral mutations can occur at random, and each of the mutations that define VOC 202012/01 had appeared before in different strains. This time, however, many mutations appeared together in the same strain, and 8 of them are in the spike protein.

Q5. Is this variant highly mutated?
Will it keep mutating?

Q5. Is this variant highly mutated? Will it keep mutating?

Like influenza viruses, SARS-CoV-2 is an RNA virus and is highly mutable, although it mutates less than influenza viruses. Because RNA viruses mutate readily, further mutation of VOC 202012/01 is probably unavoidable.

Q6. Is the new variant a major threat?
Will it spread faster or be more deadly?

Q6. Is the new variant a major threat? Will it spread faster or be more deadly?

So far we only know that its genome sequence has changed and that it has a higher multiplicative advantage than other strains in the UK. Its effective reproductive number (Rt) has also risen. However, there are no reports yet that infected people have more severe symptoms. Whether it is more deadly or affects vaccine efficacy requires close monitoring and caution. At present, we only know that it may spread faster.

Q7. Can people who have been infected
be infected again by the variant?

Q7. Can people who have been infected be infected again by the variant?

In theory, people who have recovered from infection carry multiple neutralizing antibodies (Note 2), and the variant is unlikely to evade all of them. Scientists therefore generally believe that people who have been infected are unlikely to be reinfected by the variant, but this remains to be confirmed.

Antibodies that make a virus lose its infectivity are called neutralizing antibodies. To stop the virus from entering cells, an antibody must bind to the receptor-binding domain (RBD, about 222 amino acids) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. After infection, the immune system produces antibodies against the virus's various structural proteins. Each antibody recognizes an antigenic epitope of roughly 15–20 amino acids. Because the RBD is the virus's key, antibodies that bind to it can usually neutralize the virus. Patients who have recovered from COVID-19 are known to produce neutralizing antibodies that recognize the RBD, and patients with more severe symptoms usually have higher neutralizing antibody titers. Because the RBD is fairly large (222 amino acids), antibodies can bind to different parts of it. If mutations are limited to a small part of the RBD, most antibodies whose epitopes are not at the mutated sites should still be able to neutralize the virus.

Q8. Can existing vaccines
protect against the UK variant?

Q8. Can existing vaccines protect against the UK variant?

The main component of current COVID-19 vaccines is the spike protein or its receptor-binding domain (RBD), or RNA that produces the spike protein. As with the RBD-neutralizing antibodies produced by recovered patients, vaccination triggers the immune system to produce neutralizing antibodies that recognize the RBD. Experts therefore currently believe that people vaccinated with the existing COVID-19 vaccines should be protected against the UK variant.

Q9. What is the current status of other strains?
Will they keep mutating?

Q9. What is the current status of other strains? Will they keep mutating?

RNA viruses mutate easily. The SARS-CoV-2 that now dominates the world is mainly Type VI, which evolved from the early Wuhan-1 strain, but the virus continues to mutate and new subtypes are emerging. The variant found in the UK is also a subtype of Type VI. Further mutation is therefore probably unavoidable.

Q10. Is this variant present in Taiwan now?

Q10. Is this variant present in Taiwan now?

According to the Central Epidemic Command Center and media reports on December 30, 2020, Taiwan has confirmed its first case of the UK variant, but the related genome sequence data have not yet been released. Close monitoring will continue.

Q11. How should we respond as the virus keeps mutating?

Q11. How should we respond as the virus keeps mutating?

Sun Tzu's The Art of War says: "Do not rely on the enemy not coming, but on our readiness to receive him; do not rely on the enemy not attacking, but on having made our position unassailable." In facing COVID-19, we must keep the right prevention habits and carry out all prevention measures. Advances in modern technology allow us to witness the evolution of this virus, and we believe that further research will help us understand it better and develop strategies to help control the pandemic.

英國變種新冠病毒（正式名稱：VOC 202012/01）Q&A

更新日期：2020-12-30

新冠肺炎病毒（SARS-CoV-2）對全球社會帶來巨變。本院根據全球共享流感數據倡議組織（GISAID）資料，分析二十萬筆以上的病毒基因序列樣本資料。發現病毒可歸類為六大類型。其中「第六型」自今（2020）年2、3月開始在各國急速竄升，4月已雄霸一方，佔目前流傳病毒類型的99%。

然而，病毒變異仍是現在進行式，尤其近日引發討論的英國變種病毒現在狀況如何？又會如何發展？本院除試著回答這些問題外，也將持續透過此監測網觀察新冠肺炎病毒的變異走向。

Q1. 英國變種新冠病毒來自哪裡？
最早在哪裡被發現？

Q1. 英國變種新冠病毒來自哪裡？最早在哪裡被發現？

根據英格蘭公共衛生署（Public Health England）的報告，此英國變種病毒株「VOC 202012/01」有23個病毒基因變異點。最早是在英格蘭東南方的肯特郡（Kent）所發現，起源現今仍未知。目前在英國倫敦、英國南部及東南部都有發現。

VOC 202012/01涉及的變異位點眾多，後續各單位在認定病毒株是否為VOC 202012/01時，標準不盡相同（註一）。以VOC 202012/01典型病毒株EPI_ISL_601443作為比對標準，根據全球共享流感數據倡議組織（GISAID）的265,138株病毒株基因體資料，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,354株來自英國，有3株來自丹麥，這兩國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日。發生率在英國自2020年10月底開始攀升。

根據英格蘭公共衛生署（Public Health England）的報告，此英國新變種病毒株VOC 202012/01有23個病毒基因變異點，包括參考資料中表一所列的17個變異，以及表一上方文字描述的6個變異。

表一所列的17個變異包括（以下變異名稱的描述先呈現核苷酸變異，括號內再補充氨基酸變異名稱）：

  1. ORF1ab蛋白上3個單位點（SNV）變異：C3267T（T1001I）、C5388A（A1708D）、T6954C（I2230T）。
  2. ORF1ab蛋白上1個缺失（deletion）變異：核苷酸11288 – 11296 deletion（SGF 3675 – 3677 deletion）。
  3. S蛋白上2個缺失變異：核苷酸21765 – 21770 deletion（HV 69 – 70 deletion）、核苷酸21991 – 21993 deletion（Y144 deletion）。
  4. S蛋白上6個單位點變異：A23063T（N501Y）、C23271A（A570D）、C23604A（P681H）、C23709T（T716I）、T24506G（S982A）、G24914C（D1118H）。
  5. Orf8蛋白上3個單位點變異：C27972T（Q27stop）、G28048T（R52I）、A28111G（Y73C）。
  6. N蛋白上2個核苷酸變異：28280 GAT->CTA（D3L）、C28977T（S235F）。

另外，表一上方文字描述的6個同義單位點變異（synonymous SNV）包括：

  1. ORF1ab上5個單位點變異：C913T、C5986T、C14676T、C15279T、C16176T。
  2. M基因上1個單位點變異：T26801C。

VOC 202012/01涉及的變異位點眾多，後續各單位在認定病毒株是否為VOC 202012/01時，標準未必相同。例如，GISAID上的說明是以刺突蛋白上的8變異為主。又例如，VOC 202012/01屬於GISAID上的B1.1.7支系，典型病毒株為EPI_ISL_601443（9月20日），因此也有以文件表一所列的17個變異為主。

我們根據GISAID的265,138株病毒株基因體資料來比對，若是單以S蛋白上6個單位點變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,460株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日、11月30日。若是單以S蛋白上2個缺失變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,463株來自英國，13株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、10月12日、11月30日，另外還有一株來自斯洛維尼亞，時間是3月07日（目前無法排除是否GISAID資料的問題，但若資料正確，那此變異很早就發生過）。若是單以S蛋白上2個缺失（deletion）變異加上6個單位點（SNV）變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,445株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日、11月30日。若是把文件表一所列的17個變異都納入，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,354株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日。最後，若是進一步把23個病毒基因變異點全部納入，只發現EPI_ISL_601443（9月20日）最接近，完整攜帶文件表一所列的17個變異，但6個同義單位點變異上只攜帶5個變異，在核苷酸16176位點上是攜帶C而非變異T。無法找到滿足全部23個變異的可能解釋，因為VOC 202012/01屬於GISAID上的B1.1.7支系，EPI_ISL_601443（9月20日）為該支系典型病毒株，缺少的變異會在同屬B1.1.7支系的其他病毒株中發現。

Q2. 變異是否會影響疫苗效果？

  2. M基因上1個單位點變異：T26801C。

我們根據GISAID的265,138株病毒株基因體資料來比對，若是單以S蛋白上6個單位點變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,460株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日、11月30日。若是單以S蛋白上2個缺失變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,463株來自英國，13株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、10月12日、11月30日，另外還有一株來自斯洛維尼亞，時間是3月07日（目前無法排除是否GISAID資料的問題，但若資料正確，那此變異很早就發生過）。若是單以S蛋白上2個缺失（deletion）變異加上6個單位點（SNV）變異作為標準，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,445株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，1株來自澳洲，這三國發現第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日、11月30日。若是把文件表一所列的17個變異都納入，可以發現VOC 202012/01有1,354株來自英國，3株來自丹麥，第一株的時間分別是9月20日、11月9日。最後，若是進一步把23個病毒基因變異點全部納入，只發現EPI_ISL_601443（9月20日）最接近，完整攜帶文件表一所列的17個變異，但6個同義單位點變異上只攜帶5個變異，在核苷酸16176位點上是攜帶C而非變異T。無法找到滿足全部23個變異的可能解釋，因為VOC 202012/01屬於GISAID上的B1.1.7支系，EPI_ISL_601443（9月20日）為該支系典型病毒株，缺少的變異會在同屬B1.1.7支系的其他病毒株中發現。

Q3. 我們該如何監測病毒變異？

Q3. 我們該如何監測病毒變異？

病毒在複製過程中若產生錯誤，就稱為變異。若變異對病毒無影響，則此變異就在病毒群中隨波逐流，不太起作用。若變異對病毒產生負面的影響，則此變異就會在病毒群中被淘汰。反之，若變異使病毒更適應外在環境或人體內條件，則此變異株會在人群中更易增生、傳染。大部份的變異對病毒而言都屬前兩者(無影響或負影響)。這次變異看來有更易傳播的特性，但發生原因不明。不排除是環境或人體內的正向篩選。

此外，病毒入侵細胞主要是靠病毒的刺突蛋白（Spike protein），這是病毒用來解鎖、進攻人體的鑰匙，其中刺突蛋白上的受體結合域（receptor-binding domain, RBD）是病毒最先接觸人體細胞表面的部分，若有變動，理論上便有少許可能增強病毒入侵的機率,或逃避疫苗的機會。但仍須驗證。

此次英國新變種病毒的基因共有23個變異，其中有8個（包括2個缺失(deletion)變異和6個單位點(SNV)變異）落在刺突蛋白上。6個SNV變異中又有1個在關鍵的RBD區域，也就是新冠病毒與人類細胞的結合區，是病毒進入宿主細胞的關鍵，須持續追蹤觀察。

Q4. 和過往變異病毒差別在哪？

Q4. 和過往變異病毒差別在哪？

病毒變異可能隨機發生，定義VOC 202012/01的變異之前也分別在不同病毒株出現過，但這次是眾多變異在「同一病毒株上出現」，且其中有8個落在刺突蛋白上。

Q5. 此病毒變異程度高嗎？
還會繼續變異嗎？

Q5. 此病毒變異程度高嗎？還會繼續變異嗎？

SARS-CoV-2和流感病毒同屬RNA病毒，有高變異性。但SARS-CoV-2仍比流感病毒變異性低。根據RNA病毒容易發生變異的特性，VOC 202012/01繼續變異恐難以避免。

Q6. 新變種病毒威脅大嗎？
傳染速度會因此加快嗎？變得更致命嗎？

Q6. 新變種病毒威脅大嗎？傳染速度會因此加快嗎？變得更致命嗎？

目前只知道它的基因序列改變了，且在英國的增生優勢 (multiplicative advantage) 較其他病毒株高。有效傳播數 (effective reproductive number, Rt) 亦有上升。但尚未有報導顯示感染者會有較嚴重的症狀。至於是否會更致命，或是影響疫苗效力等疑問，都須密切追縱，謹慎以對。目前僅知傳染速度可能增快。

Q7. 感染過的人還有可能會
被變異病毒感染嗎？

Q7. 感染過的人還有可能會被變異病毒感染嗎？

理論上，感染過且康復的人有多重的「中和性抗體」（註二）。變異病毒應不致於避開所有的中和性抗體。因此，目前學者們普遍認為，感染過的人應該不會被變異病毒再次感染。但是否確實如此，有待證實。

能讓病毒失去感染力的抗體，稱為中和性抗體。要阻止病毒進入細胞，抗體需結合新冠病毒刺突蛋白上的受體結合域 (稱為RBD，約有222個胺基酸) 。新冠病毒感染後，會啟動人體的免疫系統，對病毒的各種結構蛋白產生抗體。每種抗體辨識抗原的「位點」(antigenic epitope)大約是15-20個胺基酸。因為RBD是新冠病毒的鑰匙，若抗體能和RBD結合，通常能讓病毒失去感染力，而成為中和性抗體。目前已知，感染過新冠病毒的康復病人，會產生能辨識RBD的中和性抗體，而且症狀越嚴重的病人，通常產生的中和性抗體效價越高。因為RBD範圍相當大(222個胺基酸)，抗體可以結合到它的不同部位，如果突變部位僅限於RBD的局部範圍，大部分「抗原決定位(antigenic epitope)」不在該突變位置的抗體應該還會維持中和病毒的能力。

Q8. 已經研發出的疫苗可以
對抗英國變種病毒嗎？

Q8. 已經研發出的疫苗可以對抗英國變種病毒嗎？

目前新冠病毒疫苗的主要成分就是刺突蛋白或受體結合域(RBD)，或產生刺突蛋白的RNA。與康復病人產生RBD中和性抗體原理相同，接種疫苗後，人體會啟動免疫系統，產生會辨識RBD的中和性抗體。因此專家現在認為，接種過現在的新冠疫苗，應該可以對抗英國變種病毒。

Q9. 其他病毒株目前變異的狀況？
會繼續變異嗎？

Q9. 其他病毒株目前變異的狀況？會繼續變異嗎？

RNA病毒具有容易發生變異的特性，目前主宰世界的新冠病毒主要是從早期武漢一號病毒變異來的第6型，但是新冠病毒仍持續發生變異中，正在產生新的子型。此次英國發生的變異株，也是第6型的子型。因此，繼續變異恐難以避免。

Q10. 臺灣現在有沒有這株變異病毒？

Q10. 臺灣現在有沒有這株變異病毒？

根據2020年12月30日指揮中心的資訊及媒體報導，臺灣出現首例英國變種病毒，但相關基因定序資料尚未公布。後續仍需持續密切監控與觀察。

Q11. 面對病毒持續變異，我們該怎麼辦?
